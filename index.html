The Nature of the Field

Philosophy (φιλοσοφία): Literally "love of wisdom"; often defined as the study of the fundamental nature of knowledge, reality, and existence.

Plurality of Meaning: Philosophy has been understood differently across eras. Ancient lifestyle schools emphasized how to live; medieval thinkers often treated philosophy as a partner to theology; modern philosophers pursued autonomous methods for discovering fundamental truths; and twentieth-century thinkers disagreed over whether philosophy clarifies science and logic or operates as a theoretical extension of scientific inquiry.

Arguments and Logic

Argument: A set of sentences given in support of a conclusion, not merely a fight or disagreement. A premise provides support; the conclusion is the claim being supported.

Logical consistency: Statements are consistent when it is possible for all of them to be true at the same time. Consistency is not the same as truth. False statements can be mutually consistent, while an inconsistent theory cannot be true as a whole.

Fallacies and Biases

Fallacy: An error in reasoning. Formal fallacies concern argumentative structure; informal fallacies concern the relationship between premises and conclusion. Argumentum ad hominem attacks the arguer rather than the argument.

Confirmation bias or my-side bias is the unconscious tendency to seek or selectively recall information that supports an existing belief. Greater knowledge can supply more resources for defending a favored view. Belief bias occurs when agreement with a conclusion distorts one’s assessment of the argument’s strength.
